# Layout Composition

On their own, the layout primitives don't say much. Their value comes from
composition: `<jh-layout-flex>` is a flex container whose `orientation` sets its
direction, `<jh-layout-spacer>` fills the space between its children, and because
a flex container is itself a valid flex child, they nest freely.

The recipes below are named after the UI pattern they produce rather than the
components they use — that's how you'll reach for them.

## Toolbar / split header

The canonical spacer pattern: a title on the left with actions pushed to the
right by a single flexible `<jh-layout-spacer>`.

<Canvas of={stories.Toolbar} />

## Card layout (nested stacks)

A vertical `<jh-layout-flex>` whose rows are horizontal `<jh-layout-flex>`s. Each
header/footer row can use its own `justify`, `align`, and `spacer` — nesting
works because a flex container is just another flex child.

<Canvas of={stories.CardLayout} />

## Form row

A vertical `<jh-layout-flex>` of labeled horizontal `<jh-layout-flex>`s.
`align="baseline"` keeps labels and values sitting on a shared text baseline.

<Canvas of={stories.FormRow} />

## Wrapping

A horizontal `<jh-layout-flex>` with `wrap` so its children reflow onto new lines
when the container runs out of room. Resize the canvas to see the reflow.

<Canvas of={stories.Wrapping} />

## Larger form

A vertical `<jh-layout-flex>` of stacked `<jh-input>`s, followed by a horizontal
`<jh-layout-flex>` placing two distinct `<jh-checkbox-group>`s side by side,
and a spacer-aligned action row. Shows the primitives scaling up to a full form.

<Canvas of={stories.Form} />
