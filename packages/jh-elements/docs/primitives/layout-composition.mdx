# Layout Composition

On their own, the layout primitives don't say much. Their value comes from
composition: `<jh-layout-hstack>` and `<jh-layout-vstack>` are flex containers,
`<jh-layout-spacer>` fills the space between their children, and because each
stack is itself a valid flex child, they nest freely.

The recipes below are named after the UI pattern they produce rather than the
components they use — that's how you'll reach for them.

## Toolbar / split header

The canonical spacer pattern: a title on the left with actions pushed to the
right by a single flexible `<jh-layout-spacer>`.

<Canvas of={stories.Toolbar} />

## Card layout (nested stacks)

A `<jh-layout-vstack>` whose rows are `<jh-layout-hstack>`s. Each header/footer
row can use its own `justify`, `align`, and `spacer` — nesting works because a
stack is just another flex child.

<Canvas of={stories.CardLayout} />

## Form row

A `<jh-layout-vstack>` of labeled `<jh-layout-hstack>`s. `align="baseline"`
keeps labels and values sitting on a shared text baseline.

<Canvas of={stories.FormRow} />

## Wrapping

An `<jh-layout-hstack>` with `wrap` so its children reflow onto new lines when
the container runs out of room. Resize the canvas to see the reflow.

<Canvas of={stories.Wrapping} />

## Larger form

A `<jh-layout-vstack>` of stacked `<jh-input>`s, followed by an
`<jh-layout-hstack>` placing two distinct `<jh-checkbox-group>`s side by side,
and a spacer-aligned action row. Shows the primitives scaling up to a full form.

<Canvas of={stories.Form} />
